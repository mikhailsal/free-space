# V1 measurement campaign and leaf SQL experiment

The W1 harness runs successfully on GitHub at 7,297,076 entries. The prepared
W3 combination is **not promoted**: it misses the owner's fixed 15% speed-only
floor at both observed scales. No reduction in required RAM or peak disk is
demonstrated. Further W3 confirmation jobs are stopped.

The owner approved W3 within the existing six aggregate runner-hours (D17).
Completed jobs consumed **8,118 seconds = 2 h 15 min 18 s**, including builds,
setup, failed/cancelled jobs and simultaneous jobs. **13,482 seconds = 3 h 44 min
42 s** remain. No benchmark job is active. Unused time does not authorize another
candidate's campaign automatically.

Evidence: [machine-readable results](v1-w1-w3-campaign-2026-10-31.json),
[original receipts archive](v1-w1-w3-campaign-2026-10-31.tar.gz), and
[job ledger](v1-w1-campaign-2026-10-31.json). The archive contains original result
files and GitHub job receipts, with a SHA-256 index; it excludes binaries and
multi-gigabyte databases. The result JSON records the archive hash.

## Workload and measurement meaning

Both arms run on one ephemeral Ubuntu VM, from verified copies of the same two
seed generations. A fresh cgroup measures one CLI command after cache flushing;
a separate read-only observer checks the closed result. Order alternates for
three-pair screening. Current and previous snapshots remain charged to app disk.

The large synthetic fixture has 7,264,383 files and 32,693 directories, mixed
depth/fanout, long names, allocated/sparse files and hardlinks. Both large runs
completed all 7,297,076 expected entries without reported errors or incompleteness.
The fixture matches local entry count approximately, not local content or timing.

The `local` I/O sensitivity profile enforces 32 MiB/s reads, 32 MiB/s writes,
2,200 read IOPS and 3,600 write IOPS. In the full candidate job, direct-I/O checks
observed 31.98 MiB/s reads, 31.98 MiB/s writes, 2,199 read IOPS and 3,599 write IOPS.
This is an enforced synthetic envelope informed by local workload observations;
it is not a measurement of the local device's maximum speed or an exact replica.
The local scan's 1,779-second observation cannot be predicted from GH wall time.

Application disk peaks are sampled lower bounds at a requested 20 ms interval.
RSS is the measured CLI process's peak, excluding the observer. Cgroup memory
also includes charged filesystem cache and kernel memory. A successful trial
under a 4 GiB cap establishes a tested successful budget, not the minimum needed.

## W3 results

The candidate skips a child query and a redundant final aggregate UPDATE for
known non-directories. Directory handling, parent folding, publication and
hardlink attribution retain their baseline behavior.

| Workload | Baseline | Candidate | Paired outcome |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1m-file scan, 3 pairs | median 61.958 s | median 53.163 s | gains 14.29%, 15.85%, 13.13%; paired median **14.29%** |
| 1m-file snapshot, unchanged 100-entry folder refresh, 3 pairs | median 10.633 s | median 10.621 s | changes -0.12%, -0.53%, +0.68%; paired median -0.12% |
| 7.3m-entry scan, 1 exploratory pair | 534.609 s | 470.898 s | gain **11.92%**; saves 63.712 s |
| Large scan process RSS | 23.113 MiB | 23.391 MiB | +284 KiB / +1.20%; no memory benefit |
| Large scan sampled app disk peak | 3.133194 GiB | 3.133183 GiB | -12 KiB; no meaningful disk benefit |
| Large scan cgroup peak / cap | 4 GiB / 4 GiB | 4 GiB / 4 GiB | ceiling-censored; no minimum-budget or memory-saving claim |

Percentages are calculated within pairs; a ratio of separate medians is not
substituted for the paired median. The 1m RSS median paired change was +0.52%;
disk peaks were equal within each 1m pair. Refresh showed no useful speed or
resource improvement at the tested folder size.

Large-scan phase times were walk 365.46 -> 358.13 s, insert 153.28 -> 100.78 s,
index 15.73 -> 11.86 s. The insertion change is consistent with reducing SQL work,
but walk remains the largest phase. These single-pair phase differences do not
prove causality for every difference or establish W4's future benefit. Independent
SELECT-only and UPDATE-only counterfactuals and diagnostic statement counts were
not measured; no separate mechanism is accepted from the combined trial.

At the end of the large commands, baseline/candidate `memory.stat` attributed
about 1.22/1.22 GiB to file cache and 2.24/2.24 GiB to kernel memory, mainly
reclaimable slab. Those are endpoint values, not an attribution of the instant
of peak memory. Both commands had zero swap/OOM and recorded reclaim/pressure.
The low process RSS therefore does not mean the whole workload requires 24 MiB.

## Controls and remaining uncertainty

| Control | Observed elapsed deltas | Other evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1m-file scan, 3 identical-binary pairs | max absolute 0.682 s; median -0.115 s | max absolute RSS 332 KiB; sampled disk delta 0 |
| 1m-file snapshot, small-folder refresh, 3 identical-binary pairs | max absolute 0.100 s; median -0.015 s | max absolute RSS 248 KiB; sampled disk delta 0 |
| Large scan, 1 identical-binary pair | 522.921 -> 532.228 s (+1.78%) | RSS +148 KiB; sampled app disk about 3.13 GiB |
| Latest 100k-file native smoke, 1 identical-binary pair | 2.729 -> 2.727 s | sampled disk 36.39 -> 43.21 MiB (+18.77%) |

Three full-scale controls and three full-scale candidate confirmation pairs are
absent. Small-scale/native uncertainty is not transferred to large/limited-I/O
measurements. The smoke's sampled disk spread is an unresolved measurement issue;
it is not evidence of a product change between identical binaries. It prevents
a precise disk-parity claim from that smoke and motivates sampling-sensitivity
work before any disk-saving promotion. One valid pair is not qualification.

The W3 candidate also has a positive RSS delta and no proven minimum successful
memory budget. The strict no-regression rules cannot pass from an absence of
statistical significance. The independent speed floor already fails, so spending
more time to seek a passing W3 median is not justified.

## Next work

W1 infrastructure is implemented, with useful screening evidence; full resource
qualification and sampling sensitivity remain incomplete. W2 has an operations
runbook draft, without a runner rehearsal. W3's current combined candidate is
rejected for promotion. W4 should compare a bounded child-batching candidate
against the unchanged product baseline, without silently stacking rejected W3
changes or increasing SQLite cache budgets. Any new runner allocation needs a
recorded scope choice; the six-hour total and metric thresholds stay unchanged.
